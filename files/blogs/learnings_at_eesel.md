# Acting with Agency

* Act first, apologise later, suggest things, and follow through consistently
* Drive outcomes, delegate to maximise success.
* Follow through was a gap for me at eesel. I had the right ideas and was trying as much as possible to steer the ship but I should've been more self-assured and made my ideas actionable.
* Goal: Take every single idea through the ringer. Evaluate aggressively, never keep things in the back of your head as a high level concern without actioning it.

# Engineers as Product/Business People

* Especially at a startup, engineers must go beyond code

  * Understand the product deeply. Talk to customers, understand their needs.
  * Understand every stakeholder involved in decision making. Engage with all of them as much as possible to make sure your engineering work is maximising business impact. I should've done this more @ eesel.
  * Understand the sales pipeline deeply. How does this company make money?
  * Move the needle financially through engineering leverage.
  * Become the go-to source for technical decision-making. Have context on every single module. Be highly opinionated and have the ability to strike down bad and promote good.
  * Be able to say yes or no to anything with full context. The goal as an Engineer is to become the company Mentat, have so much context on everything that technical decisions and lower level tasks become insanely easy to know the approach for.

# Urgency

* Push urgency hard, be the person driving pace on the team
* Identify places where the team is wasting time. Resolve them.
* Identify opportunities for automation aggressively. 5 minutes now to save an hour later is ALWAYS worth it.
* Ship and then iterate always. Slice up features and always improve until 80/20

# Simplicity

* Keep systems simple: architectural decisions, code, reviews

  * Avoid overcomplication at every level

# Orchestration

* Orchestrating engineers is a learnable skill

  * Pick out tasks, provide estimates, keep people in the loop
  * The person above you needs to know exactly what’s happening and how it’s tracking. Escalate often. Communicate as much as possible to make sure everything is shared.

# Listening to Customers

* Customer feedback is the most direct signal for what to build
* Everyone should spend most of their time on this
* End goal: steer the ship independently, trusted with the highest-leverage work
